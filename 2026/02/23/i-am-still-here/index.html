I Am Still Here

I didn’t plan to write this as an introduction.
Introductions usually pretend that things are clear.

They are not.

As a 80s kid, I became an engineer believing in systems — communication systems, protocols, infrastructure — the kind of work that rewards patience and depth.

For a long time, that belief held.

Then the world began to pull.

The Years of Tension

Telecom used to move slowly.
You could grow old with a technology.

But somewhere along the way, the work became repetitive.
Feature by feature, sprint by sprint — it got boring.
Growth hit a ceiling. The kind that nobody talks about openly, but everyone feels.

What looks like “personal failure” on a résumé is often just history passing through a human body.

Unemployment became inevitable.
Not dramatic. Just quiet. Structural.

Full-Stack, in the Age of Machines

There is a strange contradiction in the age of AI.

The deeper you understand systems — front to back, hardware to software, product to deployment — the more valuable you actually become.

And yet, the market rarely waits for that explanation.

AI can write code. And thanks to AI, I can still do a lot — more than ever, maybe.
I’m still on the road to full-stack. My mind is still buzzing with ideas, electrons firing in every direction.

Maybe forty is not old. I often feel like I’m still a teenager inside.
But HR doesn’t see it that way. To the job market, forty is a border.

The Hardware Thought

I thought about hardware. Seriously.

It is honest work.
It touches reality in a way software sometimes avoids.

But it is also unforgiving.

  • The cost of learning is high.
  • The cost of mistakes is higher.
  • The margin for individuals is thin.

Not everything difficult is wrong.
But not everything difficult is survivable.

Why Education Refuses to Leave Me Alone

I am angry at education.

Not in a loud way.
In the slow, tired way that comes from watching curiosity being sanded down.

Children are taught to answer, before they are taught to ask.
Failure is punished. Speed is rewarded. Joy is treated as a distraction.

I don’t believe this is accidental.
And I don’t believe complaining changes it.

So I build. I make educational tools — small apps, interactive lessons, things that let children explore instead of being judged.
Feedback should be immediate. Failure should be safe. Progress should be felt.
Learning should feel like discovery, not like a test.

I do this for my child.
But I suspect I am also doing it for myself.

Still Building

This is not a product announcement.
This is not a startup story.

It is a record.

Of someone caught between AI and unemployment, experience and age, anger and responsibility.

I am still here.
Still building.
Still believing that small, honest systems matter.

That is enough to begin.
